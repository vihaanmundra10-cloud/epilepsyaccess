Neurologists & Hospitals in NJ

How to use this page: The facilities below marked "NAEC Level 4 Epilepsy Center" are verified against the National Association of Epilepsy Centers' accreditation records and each hospital's own website — these are the highest-level, most heavily-vetted epilepsy programs in New Jersey. The wider hospital directory further down is a general list of NJ acute-care hospitals sourced from the NJ Hospital Association; it is not confirmed to have epilepsy-specific programs, so call ahead to check. We intentionally do not list specific insurance networks or dollar prices per hospital here — that information changes by plan and by procedure and isn't reliably available in one place, so making it up would risk sending you somewhere your coverage doesn't work. Instead, every card links to the hospital's own insurance and price transparency pages so you get the real, current number.

Insurance & Cost of Care

General, verifiable information only — always confirm specifics with your plan and the hospital's billing office before scheduling.

Insurance

  • Under federal law (EMTALA), any hospital emergency department must screen and stabilize you regardless of insurance or ability to pay — this applies statewide, including during a seizure emergency.
  • All five Level 4 centers below are part of large academic health systems (RWJBarnabas Health, Hackensack Meridian Health, Cooper University Health Care) that typically contract with major NJ carriers such as Horizon BCBS, Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, and NJ FamilyCare/Medicaid and Medicare — but network status varies by your specific plan tier, not just the carrier name.
  • The only reliable way to confirm your plan is accepted for epilepsy/neurology specifically is to call the number on your insurance card and ask them to verify against the hospital's NPI, or call the hospital's own insurance-verification line (numbers are on each card below).

Cost of care

  • Since 2021, federal law has required every U.S. hospital to publish a machine-readable file of its standard charges and a consumer "shoppable services" price estimate tool — these are the only accurate, hospital-specific numbers available, and they update independently of anything we could list here.
  • We deliberately don't publish per-procedure epilepsy prices on this page, since costs vary hugely by test (routine EEG vs. inpatient video-EEG monitoring vs. epilepsy surgery), your insurance, and your deductible already met that year.
  • Each facility card below links directly to that hospital's official price-transparency tool.
  • For cost help, the Epilepsy Foundation maintains a 24/7 helpline and financial-assistance resource list, and every NJ hospital's financial counseling office can screen you for charity care.
